# Standing personas for lesson illustrations

Six composite people. Every illustration in the service uses one of them, labeled as an illustration ("Say you..."). None is a real person. None is Mark. Keep each one's situation consistent across lessons so the reader recognizes them.

| Persona | Situation | Typical outputs | Where they show up best |
|---|---|---|---|
| The solo consultant | One-person advisory practice, five or six clients, writes proposals and monthly reports | proposals, client emails, research memos | Lessons 1, 4, 7, 13 |
| The practice owner | Two-person accounting firm, forty clients, seasonal crunch | client letters, tax-planning memos, the who-we-are page | Lessons 6, 8, 12 |
| The sales manager | Runs a team of eight at a mid-sized software company | pipeline summaries, decks for the VP, post-call notes | Lessons 2, 10, 14 |
| The nonprofit director | Small staff, board of nine, grant deadlines | grant narratives, board reports, donor posts | Lessons 3, 9, 11 |
| The first-year founder | Two co-founders, pre-revenue, building a product | the brief, the pitch deck, the first agent | Lessons 5, 14, 15 |
| The teacher | High-school department head, lesson plans, parent communication | lesson plans, parent emails, a department style guide | Lessons 1, 4, 8 |

Rules: no names (use the role); no real companies, cities, or products; numbers are plausible and round; the same persona never contradicts an earlier lesson's facts about them. If an illustration needs a detail the table doesn't have, add it here first.
