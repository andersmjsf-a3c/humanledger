# Understand a word everyone keeps using

## The rule

If you can't explain a term in one or two plain sentences, you don't have it yet, and most of the people using it don't either.

AI vocabulary has two problems. Some of it is borrowed from engineering and means something precise that nobody tells you. Some of it is marketing and means nothing in particular. Both kinds get used in meetings as if everyone knows, and most people nod. You don't need the engineering, and you should ignore the marketing. You need about twenty-five words, each defined so you could repeat the definition to a colleague without looking.

That's what the glossary on this site is. Every term in one or two sentences, in plain words, with a link to the page where it matters. Terms are added when they earn a place, not when they appear in a press release. Retired names are kept, with a note, so that when you read an article from last year telling you to open a feature that no longer exists under that name, you know you aren't the one who's confused.

## Try it now

Pick the word you've been nodding at. Look it up in the glossary. Then, without looking, say the definition out loud to no one. If you can, you have it. If you can't, read it again, and then ask your AI:

> Explain [the term] to me in two sentences, in plain words, with one example from ordinary office work. Don't use any other technical term in the explanation.

Compare what it says to the glossary. If they disagree, the glossary was written by people and checked; the tool was answering from the average of what it has read. Trust the one with a date on it, and tell us if you think we got it wrong.

## The ones people ask about first

**Skill.** Instructions you write once for a repeat task, so next time you just name the task. It is never finished; every correction you'd otherwise repeat goes into it.

**Memory.** Things the tool remembers about you between conversations, once you've told it and it has saved them.

**Project.** A separate room with its own instructions and files, so work on one thing doesn't bleed into another.

**Hallucination.** When the tool states something false with full confidence. It fills a gap with what sounds right, and it sounds exactly the same as when it's right.

**Agent.** An AI with a job, a name, tools it's allowed to use, and a boundary it can't cross. It runs without you in the room and reports back.

**Model.** The AI itself, the thing doing the thinking. Models have names and versions, and the one you're talking to changes over time.

**Context.** What the tool knows about you and the task at the moment it answers. You supply most of it.

**Prompt.** What you type to it. The whole message, not a magic phrase.

The full glossary has the rest, including *artifact*, *brief*, *draft*, *AI tell*, *source*, *web search*, *standard*, *the three-minute test*, *vibe coding*, *coding agent*, *harness*, and the retired name *Cowork*.
