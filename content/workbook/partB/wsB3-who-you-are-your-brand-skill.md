# WS B3. Who you are (your brand skill)

A page that says who your company is, and a set of rules derived from it that the tool checks every output against. When you finish you'll have a brand skill whose first section is written in your words and whose colors come last. About an hour, plus whatever time it takes to agree on the first five sentences if you have partners.

## Why this matters

Three sentences carry the argument. Brand is who your company is and what it stands for. The guidelines, the colors, the type, the voice rules, are developed from that, and never the other way around. A company that starts with the palette ends up with a consistent picture of nothing in particular.

This is the one thing the tool cannot supply. It can produce a decent palette in a second and confident copy about anyone. What it can't do is know what you stand for, because no training data holds it. It lives in you and the people you work with. If you don't present it to the platform in a form it can use, the platform fills the gap with the average of every company it has ever read about, and you get a deck that could be anyone's.

Most companies that have a brand guide have the second half of this page and not the first. The guide has the colors and none of the reasons, and the reasons are in people's heads, which is the one place the tool can't read. So this worksheet starts where the guide should have: the page that says who we are. The colors are question nine.

## An example

*An illustration, not a real person.* The top of a two-person accounting practice's brand skill, condensed.

```
# Skill: Who [the practice] is, and the rules that follow
This skill serves [the partners]. Read the first section before anything
else. Check every document, letter, page, and post against this file
before showing it to anyone.

## Who we are
We are two accountants who answer the phone. The long version, said the
same way every time: we do the books and the taxes for forty
owner-operated businesses, and we're the people they call before they
make a decision with money in it.
We serve owners who can't afford a surprise.
We lead with the number and what it means. We don't dress up bad news
and we don't bury it.
We never promise a result we don't control.
We never share one client's situation with another, even as an example.
People who deal with us should feel that someone careful is paying
attention.

## Because of who we are
- Because we lead with the number: every letter opens with the figure
  and what it means, before any explanation.
- Because we serve owners who can't afford a surprise: every memo ends
  with "what this means for you" in two lines, and a date.
- Because someone careful is paying attention: the look is plain. One
  dark blue, black type, white paper, nothing decorative.

## The visible layer
- Colors: one dark blue for headings; black text; nothing else.
- Type: the same plain serif in every document.
- Name: "[the practice]," exactly, never abbreviated.
- Files: client name, then date, then document type.

## The check, before delivering anything
1. Read the output against "Who we are" first. Does it sound like
   people who answer the phone? Is there a promise we don't control?
2. Then the visible layer.
3. List anything that doesn't match. Don't fix it silently.
```

That check at the bottom is the piece most brand guides never have. Every design rule has a "because," and the because is a sentence about who you are. When a new question comes up, the answer comes from the first section, not from taste.

## Your turn

If you don't have a company, do this for yourself. You have a brand whether you've named it or not, and the tool is making those decisions for you right now.

1. **We are.** One sentence that says what you are, in words a customer would use. Then the longer version, two sentences, said the way you'd say it every time.
   _____

2. **We serve.** Who, specifically, and what they can't afford.
   _____

3. **We believe.** One or two sentences about how you do the work. What you lead with.
   _____

4. **We never.** Two or three things you won't do, even when it would be easier or would win the deal. Make them real. You'll be held to them.
   _____

5. **People who deal with us should feel.** One sentence. Finish it however you'd finish it.
   _____

Stop here and read answers 1 through 5 out loud. If any of them would be true of your competitor, rewrite it until it's only true of you. Don't make them sound good. Make them true.

6. **Because of who we are, our voice is.** Two or three rules, each starting with "Because" and one of your five sentences. ("Because we serve people in a hurry, every document opens with the decision.")
   _____

7. **Because of who we are, we never put on a page.** Two or three rules in the same form. Claims, names, words, tones.
   _____

8. **Because of who we are, the look is.** One or two sentences about the feel, before any color. Restrained, warm, plain, loud. Say why.
   _____

9. **The visible layer.** Now the colors, with hex codes if you have them (one main, one accent, what's never allowed); the fonts for documents and for marketing; how the company name is written, exactly; your file naming convention. If you don't have these yet, write "to be derived from 8" and come back; the tool can propose a palette from answer 8, and you'll be choosing from options that already fit.
   _____

Paste the frame below with your answers and ask the tool to tidy the wording without changing the meaning. Save it as `brand.md`.

```
# Skill: Who [company] is, and the rules that follow
This skill serves [your name]. Read "Who we are" before anything else.
Check every document, slide, page, and post against this file before
showing it to me.

## Who we are
[answers 1 to 5, as five to seven sentences]

## Because of who we are
[answers 6, 7, 8, each as a "Because..." rule]

## The visible layer
[answer 9]

## The check, before delivering anything
1. Read the output against "Who we are" first. Does it sound like us?
   Is there a claim we can't back?
2. Then the visible layer: colors, type, name, files.
3. List anything that doesn't match. Don't fix it silently.
4. If something I ask for breaks a rule above, say so before making it.
```

**Test it once.** Load the skill and ask for something small you'd actually use: a one-page summary, three slides, a short bio. Read the result against the first section before you look at the colors. The question is whether it sounds like a company that believes what you wrote in answer 3.

**Check before it leaves.** Nothing leaves your hands unchecked, and brand is where unchecked output does its quietest damage: nothing is wrong, exactly, it just isn't you. Before anything from this skill goes to a customer or a room, run the three-minute test on the who-we-are page itself: put away the materials and explain your argument, succinctly and convincingly, in three minutes or less. Can you say who your company is that way, with no slide? If you can't, the first section isn't finished, and no color fixes that.

*How to: the attached guide. If you already have a brand or style guide, don't paste it into this skill. Keep it as a reference file in the project (Part 0, worksheet 0.4) and point to it from "The visible layer." A file in the conversation is information the tool may consult. The rules in this skill are instructions it follows. Most people take a year to learn the difference.*

## Version 2, after a week of real use

Come back after the skill has produced five or six things and answer:

- What did it get wrong that no rule covered? Write the rule, and write the "because" first.
- Did any "because" turn out to be taste rather than identity? Cut it or move it to the visible layer.
- Did someone on your team disagree with a sentence in "Who we are"? That disagreement is worth more than the rest of the page. Settle it, in words, and update the file.
- Did it ever say "this breaks a rule" before making something? If never, the check isn't firing. Make it the first line of the skill.

## Why this compounds

Every output the tool makes for anyone outside your company now gets read against who you are before it leaves. Your post skill in B6 checks against this file instead of carrying its own copy. The deck you build in C5 is scored against it alongside the outside standard. And the brief in C1, the one that becomes the business, opens with these five sentences, because the brief is the business and this page is who the business is.
